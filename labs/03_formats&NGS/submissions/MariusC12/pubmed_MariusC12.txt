ARTICLE 1
Title: [Treatment of acute myeloid leukemia in older adults].
Authors: Goichi Yoshimoto
Abstract:
Acute myeloid leukemia (AML) predominantly affects older adults, who experience worse treatment outcomes than younger patients. This disparity reflects not only adverse disease biology, including complex karyotype, TP53 mutations, and myelodysplasia-related gene abnormalities, but also the cumulative impact of patient-related vulnerabilities such as comorbidities, organ dysfunction, frailty, and cognitive impairment. Accordingly, treatment selection in older AML should not be based solely on chronological age, but should incorporate fitness assessment, genetic risk, quality of life, and patient preferences within an individualized treatment framework. In recent years, venetoclax plus azacitidine has become the standard of care for patients deemed unfit for intensive chemotherapy; however, its therapeutic benefit remains limited in certain subgroups, particularly TP53-mutated disease. In addition, vigilant management of myelosuppression and infectious complications is essential throughout the treatment course. Furthermore, because treatment tolerance and therapeutic goals vary substantially among older individuals, treatment intensity should be balanced carefully against anticipated benefit and toxicity. With increasing consideration of molecularly targeted therapies and bridging strategies for allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, optimizing treatment in older AML requires rapid genomic assessment, appropriate supportive care, and shared decision-making tailored to each patient's clinical and biological characteristics.

ARTICLE 2
Title: Genomic characteristics, survival, and prognostic analysis of advanced non-small cell lung cancer with KRAS mutations and brain metastases.
Authors: Yi Chen, Xiheng Yu, Mengqi Bao, Xiaoqing Yu, Yuwei Li, Jing Sun, Linjing Zhou, Yunfeng Tong, Yun Fan
Abstract:
BACKGROUND: The brain is a common site of metastasis in lung cancer. However, data on the incidence of brain metastases (BM) and survival outcomes among patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) harboring KRAS mutations, particularly those receiving tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), remain limited. This single-center retrospective study aimed to characterize the incidence of BM in patients with KRAS-mutant NSCLC and identify prognostic factors associated with survival.
METHODS: A total of 4,021 patients with advanced NSCLC hospitalized at Zhejiang Cancer Hospital between January 2017 and December 2024 were screened. The primary endpoint was overall survival (OS), defined as the interval from BM diagnosis to death or the last follow-up. Survival curves were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. Prognostic factors associated with OS were evaluated using univariable and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression analyses.
RESULTS: Among 301 patients with KRAS-mutant NSCLC, 57 (18.9%) had BM at initial diagnosis, and 91 (30.2%) developed BM overall. The incidence of BM according to KRAS subtypes was 36.1% (39/108) for G12C, 26.3% (10/38) for G12D, 32.4% (12/37) for G12V, and 38.1% (8/21) for G12A; no statistically significant difference was observed among these subtypes. At the follow-up cutoff date of March 5, 2026, the median OS of all patients with KRAS-mutant NSCLC was 23.5 months [95% confidence interval (CI): 21.6-29.2 months]. Among patients with BM, the median OS was 18.6 months (95% CI: 15.9-23.9 months). No significant difference in OS was observed between patients with KRAS G12C and those with other KRAS subtypes (22.4 vs. 17.4 months, P=0.18). After BM diagnosis, 70 patients received immunotherapy and 19 received TKIs. TKI therapy was not associated with an OS benefit among patients with KRAS G12C mutations (22.4 vs. 23.9 months, P=0.71). Patients with TP53 co-mutations had significantly longer OS than those without TP53 co-mutations (31.9 vs. 16.3 months, P=0.03). Patients receiving immunotherapy also had significantly longer OS (22.3 vs. 10.9 months, P<0.001). In addition, brain stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS) was associated with improved survival (23.9 vs. 13.7 months, P=0.02). Multivariate analysis identified age, immunotherapy, brain SRS, and Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) as independent predictors of OS (all P<0.05).
CONCLUSIONS: BM is associated with an unfavorable prognosis in patients with KRAS-mutant NSCLC. TP53 co-mutations and higher diagnosis-specific graded prognostic assessment (DS-GPA) scores were associated with improved OS. Immunotherapy and brain SRS were significantly associated with prolonged survival.

ARTICLE 3
Title: The role of the p53 signaling pathway in prostate cancer.
Authors: Rui Shi, Hongbing Fu, Xinye Wang, Changyi Ye, Xinyun Ji, Haonan Zhao, Yuyan Zhang, Dihui Fan
Abstract:
The p53 signaling pathway is pivotal for maintaining genomic stability and regulating cell fate, playing a critical role in the initiation, progression, and treatment resistance of prostate cancer (PCa). This review provides a comprehensive overview of the p53 pathway's structure and function, with emphasis on the distribution characteristics and clinical implications of TP53 mutations in PCa. We systematically examine how these mutations correlate with disease aggressiveness, therapeutic resistance, and patient prognosis. In particular, the article delves into the mechanisms by which inactivation of the p53 pathway drives immune evasion, metabolic reprogramming, and alterations in the tumor microenvironment. We also highlight the synergistic interactions between p53 alterations and other key molecular events, including TMPRSS2-ERG fusion and PTEN loss, which together form a PCa-specific regulatory network that accelerates carcinogenesis and promotes lineage plasticity. Furthermore, the review discusses the emerging role of mutant p53 in shaping the immunosuppressive microenvironment and metabolic adaptations that underlie resistance to androgen deprivation therapy, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy. Finally, we address potential strategies and research challenges in targeting the p53 pathway for PCa treatment, including the development of small molecule reactivators, synthetic lethal approaches, and biomarker-directed combination therapies. This review offers a theoretical foundation and guidance for future basic research and clinical applications aimed at improving outcomes for patients with PCa.

ARTICLE 4
Title: Longitudinal 
Authors: Dan Zhao, Deyang Xie, Kun Li, Nana Zhang, Zichen Liu, Nanying Che
Abstract:
BACKGROUND: Apparent TP53 gain or loss in repeated next-generation sequencing (NGS) may reflect technical noise, spatial heterogeneity, metachronous multiple primary tumors, or true clonal evolution. This study aimed to characterize TP53 longitudinal dynamics using a transparent verification framework.
METHODS: We analyzed 948 patients with non-small cell lung cancer undergoing at least two tissue-based NGS tests separated by ≥120 days (all specimens ≥20% tumor-cell content). Apparent TP53 flip events underwent two-step sequential verification: molecular lineage adjudication comparing complete variant spectra of sequential specimens, followed by an empirically anchored variant allele frequency (VAF) threshold (≥5%) derived from platform re-detection characteristics. Driver background and MET amplification were exploratory outcomes.
RESULTS: TP53 status remained stable in 824 patients (86.9%); 124 (13.1%) showed apparent gain (n=75) or loss (n=49). Lineage adjudication excluded 54 patients (5.4% of 1,002 eligible) with fully discordant driver spectra, whose apparent flip rate was three-fold higher than concordant patients (29.6% vs. 9.6%). Sequential verification identified 92 high-confidence events (64 acquisitions, 28 losses), showing no driver enrichment (EGFR 65.2% vs. 65.4% in the cohort; P>0.99). MET amplification did not differ across trajectories (high-confidence acquired vs. persistent negative: 8.9% vs. 6.3%; P=0.52).
CONCLUSIONS: TP53 alterations are largely stable during longitudinal monitoring. Many apparent flips reflect metachronous multiple primaries or near-detection-limit noise and are identifiable by a simple sequential framework. After artifact control, TP53 dynamics show no EGFR preference, and previously suggested MET associations were not confirmed under uniform ascertainment.

ARTICLE 5
Title: Safety, Efficacy, and Pharmacokinetics of Unecritinib (TQ-B3101) in Pediatric Patients With Relapsed/Refractory Anaplastic Lymphoma Kinase-Positive Anaplastic Large-Cell Lymphoma: A Phase I Study.
Authors: Suying Lu, Junting Huang, Ruiqing Cai, Yan Gao, Jia Zhu, Juan Wang, Yi Que, Zijun Zhen, Feifei Sun, Huiqiang Huang, Yizhuo Zhang
Abstract:
This study evaluated the safety, pharmacokinetics (PK), and efficacy of unecritinib, a novel ROS1/anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK)/c-MET inhibitor, in pediatric patients with relapsed/refractory (R/R) ALK-positive (ALK+) anaplastic large-cell lymphoma (ALCL). This was an open-label, single-arm, Phase I study using a 3+3 dose-escalation design in patients aged 10-18 years. The primary endpoints were safety, determination of the recommended phase II dose (RP2D), and PK profiling. Secondary endpoints included overall response rate (ORR), progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS). Nine patients were evaluable for toxicity and efficacy analyses. No dose-limiting toxicities were observed. The most frequent treatment-related adverse events were neutropenia, leukopenia, vomiting, and nausea, predominantly Grade 1-2. PK analysis showed rapid metabolism of unecritinib by carboxylesterase to its active metabolite unecritinib M, with a T max of approximately 3 h. The ORR was 100% (six complete and three partial responses). The 3-year PFS and OS rates were 53.3% and 100%, respectively. Genomic alterations in TP53, MYC, KMT2D, and MYCL1 were identified in a subset of patients, suggesting potential prognostic heterogeneity. Unecritinib demonstrated a favorable safety profile and promising preliminary efficacy in patients with R/R ALK+ ALCL. The RP2D was 250 mg twice daily for patients aged > 10 years. Trial Registration: ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT04306887.
